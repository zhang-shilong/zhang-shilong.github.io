My research is driven by two complementary questions: <b>What makes us human, and how can understanding our genomes improve human health?</b>
1. <b>Primate Evolutionary Genetics</b>. Understanding what makes us human requires looking beyond our own species. I investigate the genetic changes that have shaped primate evolution, using comparative genomics to explore the origins of shared and species-specific traits. As a foundation for this work, I have generated a telomere-to-telomere (T2T) macaque reference genome, opening previously inaccessible regions of the genome to evolutionary investigation.
2. <b>Personalized Genomics and AI for Rare Disease Diagnosis</b>. Each human genome carries clues to an individual’s health, yet translating these clues into a diagnosis remains a major challenge, particularly for rare genetic diseases. I aim to bridge this gap by developing an AI agent system that integrates individual genomic variation, clinical phenotypes, and biomedical knowledge to automate diagnostic reasoning and help bring timely, precise answers to patients and their families.
